# Gas Odor Emergency Response

## 1. Scope
Applies to any report of natural gas or propane odor ("rotten eggs"), hissing from gas piping, or a gas alarm, in any building, lab or mechanical room. Gas odor is always treated as an emergency until the gas utility or a qualified technician declares the area safe.

## 2. Safety precautions
- Do NOT operate light switches, outlets, elevators, phones, radios or anything that could create a spark inside the affected area.
- Do NOT try to find the leak by sight or smell, and never use a flame.
- Evacuate upwind. Use a phone or radio only once you are outside the affected area.
- Only the utility or a qualified gas technician may re-enter before the all-clear.

## 3. Tools & parts
- Calibrated combustible gas detector (for qualified responders only).
- Barricade tape and signs.
- Building emergency contact list and gas utility emergency number.

## 4. Procedure
1. The first responder evacuates the immediate area and keeps others out. If the odor is strong or widespread, pull the fire alarm to evacuate the building.
2. From a safe location, call 911 / campus police and the gas utility emergency line.
3. Notify Environmental Health & Safety and the Work Control Center (P1).
4. If it can be done safely and the shutoff is outside the affected area, a trained technician may close the building gas service valve. Do not enter the odor area to reach a valve.
5. Keep the area barricaded until the utility or fire department clears it.
6. After the all-clear, a qualified plumber locates and repairs the leak and pressure-tests the piping before gas service is restored.
7. Relight pilots and verify appliance operation only after the repair passes testing.

## 5. Verification & closeout
- Written all-clear from fire department or utility recorded in the work order.
- Pressure test result and repair details recorded.
- Incident report submitted to Environmental Health & Safety within 24 hours.
