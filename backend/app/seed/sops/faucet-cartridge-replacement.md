# Faucet Cartridge Replacement

## 1. Scope
Applies to single-handle and two-handle lavatory faucets (AquaFlow AF-200 series and similar) that drip from the spout, leak around the handle, or will not shut off fully. A drip from the spout with the handle closed almost always means a worn cartridge or seat/spring; a leak at the base of the handle usually means a failed O-ring on the cartridge body. If the same faucet has needed service within the last 60 days, treat it as a possible repeat failure: check supply pressure and water hardness before simply swapping the cartridge again.

## 2. Safety precautions
- Shut off both hot and cold supply stops under the sink before disassembly; open the faucet to relieve pressure.
- Hot water lines in restrooms can exceed 120 °F (49 °C). Let the line cool and wear gloves.
- Place a towel or drain cover in the basin so small parts are not lost down the drain.
- If supply stops are seized or leak when turned, stop and isolate at the branch valve instead of forcing them.

## 3. Tools & parts
- Replacement cartridge matched to faucet make/model (AF-200 uses cartridge AF-C200).
- O-ring kit and silicone plumber's grease (NSF-61 rated).
- Allen key set (handle set screw is usually 2.5 mm or 3/32 in), adjustable wrench, cartridge puller.
- Flashlight, towels, small bucket, pressure gauge with hose-bib adapter (for repeat failures).

## 4. Procedure
1. Close both supply stops and open the faucet to drain the line.
2. Pry off the decorative cap, loosen the handle set screw and remove the handle.
3. Remove the retaining nut or clip that holds the cartridge.
4. Note the cartridge orientation (hot/cold stops), then pull it straight out; use a cartridge puller if it is stuck. Do not twist the faucet body.
5. Inspect the faucet body seat for scale or scoring. Clean scale with a soft brush; a scored seat may need the faucet body replaced.
6. Grease the new cartridge O-rings lightly and insert it in the same orientation as the old one.
7. Reinstall the retaining nut hand-tight plus a quarter turn, then the handle and cap.
8. Open the supply stops slowly and check for leaks under the sink and at the handle.
9. For a repeat failure, measure static pressure at the nearest hose bib. Above 80 psi, report it to the Plumbing lead for pressure-reducing valve inspection, because high pressure shortens cartridge life.

## 5. Verification & closeout
- Run hot and cold for 30 seconds each; confirm no drip from the spout after shut-off for 2 minutes.
- Confirm the handle turns smoothly and temperature mixes correctly.
- Record the cartridge part number, supply pressure (if measured) and root cause in the work order.
- Remove the old cartridge and packaging; wipe the basin and counter dry.
