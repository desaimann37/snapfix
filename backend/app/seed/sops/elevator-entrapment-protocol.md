# Elevator Out-of-Service and Entrapment Protocol

## 1. Scope
Covers elevators that are out of service, stopping between floors, leveling poorly, or have door faults, and any passenger entrapment. Campus staff do not repair elevators: the elevator contractor performs all work. Campus staff secure the area, communicate with passengers and coordinate access.

## 2. Safety precautions
- Never attempt to open hoistway or car doors or to free passengers yourself; only the elevator contractor or fire department may do this.
- Keep the passenger calm and talking; tell them help is on the way and not to try to leave the car.
- If a passenger reports a medical issue, call 911 immediately.
- Place "Out of Service" signs at every landing for a disabled elevator.

## 3. Tools & parts
- Elevator contractor 24/7 phone number, elevator ID and building address.
- Out-of-service signs and barricade tape.
- Radio or phone for passenger communication.

## 4. Procedure
1. Entrapment: confirm through the car phone or by voice that passengers are inside, how many, and whether anyone needs medical help.
2. Call the elevator contractor's emergency line with the elevator ID; for medical issues or contractor response over 30 minutes, call 911.
3. Stay at the landing nearest the car and keep in contact with passengers until they are freed.
4. Out of service without entrapment: sign all landings and confirm the car is parked and not moving.
5. Notify building occupants who rely on the elevator for accessibility, and point them to the nearest working elevator.
6. Escort the contractor to the machine room and log their arrival and departure times.

## 5. Verification & closeout
- Contractor confirms the elevator is safe and returned to service; remove signs.
- Record entrapment duration, passenger count, contractor ticket number and cause.
- Elevators with three or more faults in 30 days are flagged for a contractor engineering review.
