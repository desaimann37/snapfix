# Suspected Mold Assessment

## 1. Scope
Covers visible suspected mold growth, persistent musty odors, and chronically damp materials (carpet, drywall, ceiling tiles). Mold is almost always caused by a moisture source: roof leaks, plumbing leaks, condensation, or water entering at windows. The source must be fixed for remediation to last.

## 2. Safety precautions
- Do not disturb or dry-brush suspected mold; it releases spores.
- Wear an N95 respirator, gloves and eye protection for inspection; larger areas need EHS-approved PPE.
- Occupants with respiratory symptoms should be relocated while the assessment is completed.
- Areas larger than 10 sq ft of growth require remediation by a trained crew under EHS oversight.

## 3. Tools & parts
- Moisture meter, IR camera (if available), flashlight.
- N95 respirators, nitrile gloves, goggles.
- Plastic sheeting and tape for temporary containment; camera for documentation.

## 4. Procedure
1. Interview the reporter: when the odor or growth was first noticed, any recent leaks or flooding.
2. Inspect and photograph the affected area. Estimate the affected square footage.
3. Map moisture with the meter on walls, floors and ceilings around the area; record readings.
4. Identify the moisture source (window, roof, pipe, HVAC condensate) and open a work order for the responsible trade.
5. For less than 10 sq ft on non-porous surfaces, clean with detergent solution and dry within 24–48 hours.
6. For porous materials (ceiling tile, carpet pad, drywall) with growth, contain the area and schedule removal.
7. For more than 10 sq ft, contain the area, restrict access and engage the remediation crew through EHS.

## 5. Verification & closeout
- Moisture readings back to normal (below 15% for drywall) within 72 hours.
- Source repair work order referenced in this ticket.
- Follow-up inspection scheduled two weeks after remediation.
