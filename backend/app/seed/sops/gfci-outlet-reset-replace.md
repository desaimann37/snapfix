# GFCI Outlet Reset and Replacement

## 1. Scope
Covers dead receptacles in restrooms, labs, kitchens and exterior locations protected by GFCI devices (VoltSafe VS-20 and similar). Many "dead outlet" reports are a tripped upstream GFCI. Any outlet that is scorched, melted, sparking, or smells of burning is a hazard: de-energize it immediately and treat it as an emergency.

## 2. Safety precautions
- Treat every receptacle as live until verified with a tested meter.
- Apply lockout/tagout at the panel before removing a device.
- Never bypass a GFCI or replace it with a standard receptacle in a wet location.
- If the device shows burn marks, check the box and conductors for heat damage before installing a replacement.

## 3. Tools & parts
- Plug-in GFCI tester, multimeter, non-contact voltage tester.
- Replacement 20 A tamper-resistant GFCI receptacle (weather-resistant for exterior).
- Insulated screwdrivers, wire strippers, cover plate (in-use cover for exterior).

## 4. Procedure
1. Identify the protecting GFCI: check nearby GFCI devices on the same wall, in the same restroom, or the upstream outlet in the circuit.
2. Press RESET firmly. If it will not reset, unplug all loads on the circuit and try again; a faulty appliance can trip it immediately.
3. If the device still will not reset, or the outlet is damaged, lock out the circuit and verify zero voltage.
4. Remove the cover plate and device. Note LINE and LOAD conductors.
5. Install the new GFCI with incoming power on LINE terminals and downstream outlets on LOAD terminals. Torque terminal screws to the manufacturer's value.
6. Restore power, press RESET, and test with the TEST button and a plug-in tester.

## 5. Verification & closeout
- GFCI trips on TEST and resets; downstream outlets are protected.
- Tester shows correct wiring (no open ground, no reversed polarity).
- Record the circuit number and reason for failure (tripped, nuisance load, failed device, damage).
