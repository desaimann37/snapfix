# Clogged Drain Clearing

## 1. Scope
Covers slow or fully blocked lavatory, kitchen and floor drains. Does not cover main sewer backups (multiple fixtures backing up at once, sewage odor at floor drains); those require the Plumbing lead and possibly an outside contractor with a camera and jetter.

## 2. Safety precautions
- Never use chemical drain openers in campus fixtures; they damage traps and can splash.
- Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection; drain water may contain biological contamination.
- If more than one fixture on the same line is backing up, stop and escalate as a possible main line blockage.
- Kitchen drains may contain hot grease; let them cool.

## 3. Tools & parts
- Cup plunger (sinks) and flange plunger (toilets are covered by a separate procedure).
- 1/4 in hand auger (25 ft) for lavatory lines; 3/8 in drum auger for floor drains.
- Bucket, towels, channel-lock pliers, replacement P-trap washers.
- Enzymatic drain maintenance treatment for recurring grease issues.

## 4. Procedure
1. Remove the pop-up stopper or strainer and clear visible hair or debris.
2. Plug the overflow opening with a wet rag and plunge with 10–15 firm strokes.
3. If still slow, place a bucket under the P-trap, loosen the slip nuts and remove the trap. Clean it out.
4. Feed the hand auger into the wall stub until resistance is felt, then crank to break up or retrieve the blockage.
5. Reassemble the trap with new washers if the old ones are flattened or cracked; hand-tighten plus a quarter turn.
6. Flush with hot water for one minute.
7. For floor drains, remove the strainer, auger the line, and refill the trap with water (or trap primer) to block sewer gas.

## 5. Verification & closeout
- Fill the basin and confirm it drains in under 10 seconds with no gurgling at nearby fixtures.
- Check the trap connections for drips with a dry paper towel.
- If the same drain has clogged twice in 30 days, recommend a camera inspection in the work order.
